## Slide 2: What Is Supervised Learning?

"Supervised learning is a machine learning approach where the model learns from labeled training data. Each example in the training data has both an input and the correct output label." [ibm](https://www.ibm.com/topics/supervised-learning)

"Think of it like a teacher-student relationship. The teacher provides examples with answers, and the student learns to recognize patterns. The model studies these labeled examples and learns to map inputs to outputs." [geeksforgeeks](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/supervised-machine-learning/)

"The goal is simple: after training on labeled data, the model can predict the correct output for new, unseen inputs based on the patterns it learned during training." [ibm](https://www.ibm.com/topics/supervised-learning)

## Slide 3: Two Main Types

"Supervised learning has two main types: Classification and Regression. Understanding the difference is crucial." [ibm](https://www.ibm.com/topics/supervised-learning)

"Classification predicts discrete categories or classes. For example, deciding if an email is spam or not spam, or identifying whether an image contains a cat, dog, or bird. The output is always a class label." [geeksforgeeks](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/classification-vs-regression/)

"Regression predicts continuous numerical values. For example, predicting house prices based on features like size and location, or forecasting temperature. The output is always a number." [geeksforgeeks](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/classification-vs-regression/)

"Remember this key difference: Classification predicts categories, Regression predicts numbers." [ibm](https://www.ibm.com/topics/supervised-learning)

## Slide 5: How It Works

"The supervised learning process follows three main steps. Let's break it down." [ibm](https://www.ibm.com/topics/supervised-learning)

"First, you collect labeled data. This is the most critical step—you need examples with inputs and their correct outputs. The quality and quantity of this data directly impacts model performance." [geeksforgeeks](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/supervised-machine-learning/)

"Second, you train the model. The model processes the labeled data and learns patterns by continuously adjusting its parameters to minimize prediction errors. It's essentially finding the mathematical relationship between inputs and outputs." [ibm](https://www.ibm.com/topics/supervised-learning)

"Third, you make predictions. Once trained, the model can take new, unseen inputs and predict their outputs based on the patterns it learned during training." [geeksforgeeks](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/supervised-machine-learning/)

"The key insight here is that the quality of your labeled data determines the quality of your predictions. Better data means better models." [ibm](https://www.ibm.com/topics/supervised-learning)
